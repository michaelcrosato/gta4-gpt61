# LOWLIGHT: story coverage and source research

This is a research and design inventory for the requested full game. Every counterpart below is **planned**. A catalogue entry, mission title, or playable arrival sequence does not demonstrate a finished mission or a complete game.

The source is the [requested GTA IV article](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_IV). Direct Fandom access was blocked by robots; the search index returned the [mission catalogue](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Missions_in_GTA_IV), individual mission articles, the [character index](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Characters_in_GTA_IV), [plot article](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Plot_in_GTA_IV), and [ending article](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Endings_in_GTA_IV). Indexed text is sufficient to identify the mission-title union and broad encounter shapes, but does not certify every cinematic, line, failure condition, optional interaction, or dependency. Detailed source gaps remain explicit. Research date: 8 October 2026.

## Original identity and narrative structure

LOWLIGHT takes place in Harbor City: Breakwater, Saint Brigid, Glassward, The Narrows, and Ironhaven. Mara Voss is a former disaster-relief driver arriving after coastal civil unrest. Her cousin Felix operates a failing taxi co-op. Mara's skills make her useful to institutions that have turned emergency supplies, housing permits, evacuation routes, and floodgate access into private assets. Her central question is who profited from a relief convoy's failure, and whether she can secure a new home without selling another community's safety.

The source's experiential arcs include arrival and strained family hopes; coercive work for unstable intermediaries; betrayal and forced relocation; competing neighborhood and metropolitan interests; a large crew heist; a contested valuable-cargo chain; a confrontation with the protagonist's past; and two consequential finale routes. These are structural coverage targets. The original story uses relief contracts and civic infrastructure, distinct relationships and testimony, and a sabotaged public commissioning. It requires new dialogue, scene geography, character motives, outcomes, music, and visual staging.

Proposed act progression is an authored design rather than a claim about GTA IV's exact chronology:

1. **Arrival and dependence:** work, phone, transport, safe shelter, social activities, debt, and controlled-force tutorials.
2. **Contracts and displacement:** wider combat, unstable patrons, betrayal, shelter loss, and neighborhood alliances.
3. **City leverage:** policing, surveillance, development, crew work, heist escalation, and divided family loyalties.
4. **Authentication and memory:** contested floodgate keys, independent freight factions, institutional bargains, and former relief colleagues.
5. **The last contract:** acceptance or disclosure, a branch-specific commissioning crisis, distinct multi-vehicle finales, and persistent city consequences.

## What is counted

The union below contains **90 distinct source mission titles** across both terminal routes. The source catalogue advertises 88 story missions, but that headline must not be substituted for a union of title records. `Mr. & Mrs. Bellic` is one title with two branch-specific scenes. The source also separates optional storyline content, and [The Holland Play](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/The_Holland_Play) has a setup and a delayed decision. [The ending article](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Endings_in_GTA_IV) notes that in-game statistics use their own wedding-mission counting convention. No content is removed to force a preferred number.

The list is an inventory order. It is not a verified chronological sequence or dependency graph: parallel contacts, required phone calls, appointments, outfits, delayed requests, and optional resolutions need explicit dependency records. `No. 1`, `Ruff Rider`, `Undress to Kill`, and `The Holland Play` are retained as optional storyline content. Both Deal and Revenge source routes are covered; neither can be simulated by replacing the last mission's label.

## Per-mission production contract

A row moves from planned to implemented only when it has a distinct playable location and staged phases, original cinematic/dialogue assets, appropriate enemy placements and reactions, objective and failure conditions, meaningful rewards/unlocks, and persistent outcomes. Vehicle changes, tailing, chases, surveillance, scheduled meetings, social interactions, interior entrances, climbs, ladder/roof traversal, moving-truck boarding, and helicopter transfers cannot be replaced with a shared drive-and-shoot loop.

Each mission requires a save/checkpoint state contract, restart behavior, restored companion and vehicle state, and checks at phase boundaries. Branches require separate saved playthrough evidence, and no branch should erase unrelated optional content or corrupt free-roam activities. Finished assets and polished performance must be demonstrated, not inferred from data counts.
