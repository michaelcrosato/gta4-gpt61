# LOWLIGHT: systems and optional-content scope

This is a production requirements catalogue for the full original game described in the attached brief. It is not a completion report. Every catalogue entry is currently `planned`; none is certified as implemented or playable by this document. The companion [systems-source-map.json](systems-source-map.json) contains 869 individually identified system and content requirements, 50 references, acceptance profiles and explicit remaining research gaps.

The baseline is the original multiplatform **Grand Theft Auto IV**, including its optional content, original online play and PC additions. The two episodic expansions, promotional material and cut content are tracked as scope distinctions; an item appearing in a shared wiki category does not establish that it is usable in the base game. The supplied overview could be read through indexed excerpts, while direct article requests returned HTTP 402. Accessible guides and Rockstar's official PC guide supplement the requested wiki. The catalogue records which references were read and which were available only through search indexing. [Requested overview](https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_IV), [official PC guide](https://media.rockstargames.com/rockstargames-newsite/img/manuals/en_us/GTAIV_PC_SurvivalGuide_M01.pdf).

## Original identity and fidelity

LOWLIGHT takes place in Harbor City, across Breakwater, Saint Brigid, Glassward, The Narrows and Ironhaven. Its central design concern is a city that reacts: people and traffic possess physical continuity; relationships affect services and stories; vehicles feel heavy and consequential; criminal action creates evidence and pursuit; money and movement connect ordinary life to dangerous work.

Source identifiers are research labels, never product names. The original fleet uses new manufacturers and models. Characters, corporations, broadcast stations, websites, artwork, dialogue, music and mission situations receive original identities. A source combat encounter can inspire a comparable tactical problem without reproducing its setting, staging, dialogue or character arc. A role match alone does not justify reusing a protected character design, logo, soundtrack or script.

The full-game fidelity requirement includes breadth and authored variety. Thirty cases need thirty produced encounters, not one fight with thirty labels. A collectible needs an authored location and a valid discovery action. A minigame needs rules, input and an opponent; a menu saying “won” is insufficient. A radio channel needs recorded programming; a generated station title is insufficient. Networking must connect real clients. A vertical slice is the quality bar for producing the remaining game and cannot substitute for it.

## Interlocking gameplay requirements

The 48 system records carry explicit acceptance conditions. Their dependencies should govern development order, while their full scope remains fixed.

1. **Physical city:** collision-aware locomotion, camera, authored interiors, road topology, traffic, pedestrians, world time, weather and ambient sound. Street actors must behave meaningfully when encountered, threatened, injured or blocked. Streaming must preserve mission actors and owned vehicles.
2. **Vehicle play:** entry, seats, passengers, theft, driving, damage and saving. Cars, motorcycles, boats and helicopters require separate behaviours and animation. Heavy handling, momentum, suspension and mechanical failure should make different fleet classes matter.
3. **Combat and public response:** gunplay, ammunition, reloads, cover, melee defence/counters, damage and witness-based pursuit. Police need sight/hearing, navigation, last-known-position search, escalation, roadblocks and airborne/tactical response. Pursuit cannot be a star counter that decays regardless of world observation.
4. **Narrative objectives:** prerequisite graphs, individual encounter actors, objective ownership, dialogue, checkpoint state and consequence persistence. A package handoff proves the recipient possesses the intended item. A vehicle commission proves the correct entity arrived intact. A multi-target case accounts for every required actor.
5. **Ordinary city life:** money, food, shops, repairs, homes, transit, shows, media and communication. These must form playable scenes and usable services rather than catalogue screens. The phone and fictional internet connect authored city events and relationships.
6. **Social simulation:** friendship/date availability, schedules, invitations, preferences, multi-stop outings, conversations and earned physical services. Modernised quiet hours, reminders and sensible cancellation rules can improve friction while retaining the activities and relationships.
7. **Long-tail authored content:** distinct side jobs, cases, encounters, courses, collectible placements, stunt lines, full media production and original city businesses. Production tracking must reference each record rather than close a category once its first example works.
8. **Network and platform features:** authoritative sessions, every mode's rules, real client recovery, ranked progression, editor/export and optional personal audio. Local bots are useful for AI testing but cannot certify multiplayer.

The underlying engine remains the supplied `my-3d2dge` foundation. Gameplay simulation, objective state, physics, AI, audio, save data and networking should expose clear boundaries to the engine rather than bury rules in rendering callbacks. Fixed simulation ticks and stable actor/item identities are especially important: renderer success alone cannot demonstrate valid combat, driving, mission progression or network synchronisation. These are original engineering requirements, not a claim that the current engine already supplies them.

## Acceptance evidence and reporting

Each content record inherits a real acceptance profile and, where needed, individual mechanics. Reviewers should be able to start from a clean save, reach the item or activity, play a successful attempt, demonstrate the relevant failures, save/reload and inspect the resulting state. Evidence should identify the build, controls, device/engine, recording or automation path and observed outcome.

Statuses have deliberate meaning:

- `planned`: a requirement or proposal exists; gameplay is not claimed.
- `implemented`: concrete runtime code and original assets are linked to the record.
- `verified`: reproducible play demonstrates its acceptance criteria, including failure and persistence behaviour.

All current records are `planned`. JSON syntax, unique IDs, reference integrity and counted catalogue rows were checked. Those checks establish that the requirements file is internally coherent; they do not establish that any represented system exists in the running game.

Gameplay UI changes also need the repository's phone, tablet, wide-screen and WebKit checks, with screenshots inspected outside the repository. Controller/touch controls, HUD safe areas, menu navigation and readable text should accompany desktop gameplay tests. Real iOS Safari is not available on this machine; WebKit device profiles are its closest automated proxy.

## Research gaps that block an exhaustive claim

The JSON stores 17 named gaps with the evidence and next action for each. The most material are exact vehicle keys and cosmetic subvariants, item-category classification, throwable subvariants, route topology, individual fare definitions, source course shape, PC-extra course identification, collectible/stunt placement mapping, all performed-media content, website nested identities and final achievement thresholds.

The campaign/cast map must also be reconciled with these system roles, supporting actors, factions and location needs. Original character names in the random/case catalogues are proposals; the main campaign supplies the canonical cast IDs before implementation. No second duplicate cast should arise merely because two requirements files describe the same source role.

This catalogue therefore sets `research_complete` to `false`. The requested full game is not cleared by completing the source game's narrower 100% formula, by producing one representative slice or by matching counts. Release requires resolving the remaining source inventory, producing each original counterpart and verifying the complete playable result.
